# Pre-production review of the shipped image

## 1. Executive verdict

All 85 findings of the independent pre-production review are closed: 78 are FIXED by a named, accepted card and verified against the final re-ingested fleet, and 7 are ACCEPTED as documented, non-defect limitations with a published release note. The Round-3 independent review's 95 additional rows drove ten more cards (TC-557 to TC-571) through the same acceptance gate, including the two regressions (PS-08/RST-14's search_symbols ranking and the pdf/cpp, 3d/net and 3d/java runtime-verification note) discovered only after the Round-3 closing re-ingest and fixed by TC-571 and TC-536 in this same session. Every open question the review generated (OQ-012 through OQ-015, plus OQ-017 raised during the closing re-ingest) is resolved, and all eight supervisor work items (SUP-1 through SUP-8) are done. The fleet runs from a single image tag with a reproducible claims ledger backing every published number. The one remaining step before delivery, pushing the image to the operator's real registry, is an owner-only action (OWNER-13) that this review does not block.

Review date: 2026-10-22

## 2. Scope and method

An independent, read-only review attacked the shipped image and the repository across seven slices,
totalling 85 distinct findings. Fixtures and the project's own extractor were never used as the oracle;
upstream source, compiled libraries, reflection, the real package registries and real toolchains were.
No reproduction meant no defect, and severity followed evidence.

- **Generation, readiness and recovery (GRR)**: Generation lifecycle, readiness, publish safety, rollback and recovery, probed against all 40 pilots baked at the time of the review (now 32 after the jmap family was removed): kills at 3 publish points, flip failure, ENOSPC, guard refusals, restart and `kill -9` recovery, and concurrent-publisher fencing (30 trials).
- **Fleet and Kubernetes (FK8S)**: The live Kubernetes cluster and chart: 40 pilots (now 32) through `prove_pilots.py`'s 11 checks each (first run 25 of 40, root-caused to FK8S-02/FK8S-03), pod hardening (81 containers), restart timing and whole-cluster bounce recovery.
- **Protocol and security (PS)**: Protocol and security across 7 exercised pilots: ~770 sampled responses and 17,000+ soak calls, 600-of-600 concurrency at 20x30, 1,609 unique correlation ids, adversarial 1,000-character queries, and 7 pilots x 33 calls of HTTP-versus-stdio equivalence with zero differences.
- **Isolation and provenance (ISOPROV)**: Isolation and provenance: 103,828 cross-scope isolation requests against 40 targets (19,056 same-family, 27,408 same-platform, 54,936 other) with 0 leaks; 4,098 bypass probes on 10 pilots; 7,783 baked chunks scanned for injection content; 21 upstream clones checked line-for-line against 3,258 indexed symbols.
- **Agentic tasks (AGT)**: Agentic tasks: 65 blind coding tasks with 1,037 MCP calls across 13 blind subagents on 13 pilots, judged for sufficiency without seeing the servers' own code.
- **Examples, product reference and releases (D)**: Examples, product reference and releases across all 40 baked pilots (now 32): 55 served releases cross-checked byte-for-byte against GitHub, 34 registry verdicts re-verified, 33 of 33 served examples compiled or run in assembled form at their pinned commits across 7 languages, and 40 product-reference objects checked across 8 sections each.
- **Retrieval and source truth (RST)**: Retrieval and source truth across 19 of the 40 pilots: 390 phrased lookup queries naming an existing type, 267 task questions for existing features, 37 verified-absent-feature probes, and README-identifier recall measured per pilot against upstream clones checked out at the indexed commit.

## 4. The existing proof suite and its blind spots

The register's own proof-suite critique (slice A, fleet-k8s): the automated suite the project ran against itself before this review was fixture-circular (symbol, commit and title all come from the same fixtures the generation was built from), ran one probe per tool, treated an honest miss as a pass for `find_examples`, treated an empty list `[]` as a pass for `list_recent_changes`, did no signature comparison against upstream, ran no natural-language queries, was latency-blind at a 15 second client timeout, and trusted the server's own reported scope rather than an independent one. The escapes this allowed through, and what now closes each one:

- Wrong parameter lists and other signature-fidelity defects (no signature comparison in the old suite): closed by the per-language Round-3 fidelity cards and their golden-fixture audits (TC-532 Python, TC-533 TypeScript, TC-534 C++, TC-535 Go and Rust, TC-557 Java, TC-558 .NET), which compare extracted signatures against upstream source, not the fixtures the suite itself built from.
- Wrong-commit docs and sidecars (the old suite passed the fixture's own commit as current, so a mixed-commit generation always looked fresh): closed by TC-513 and TC-533, which re-pin pdf/typescript to one commit and re-extract, and by ISOPROV-03/FK8S-07/RST-16's shared fixture-commit-equals-chart-commit test.
- Unresolved main API dropped by the 300-type cap with no coverage caveat on the miss: closed by TC-520, TC-530 and TC-550, which add a `coverage_note` to every partial-index miss and feed README and furnished-doc identifiers into the must-keep set.
- A symbol-only generation that passes readiness because 'some lexical doc exists': closed by TC-506, which requires real floors (at least 3 symbols, 1 documentation chunk, and a generation whose scope matches the deployment) before `/readyz` answers ready.
- `find_examples` stalling on an unreachable embedding gateway (honest miss was treated as a pass, hiding a ~93 second worst case): closed by TC-508, which adds a hard deadline, removes retries, and reports an honest lexical-only fallback instead of hanging.
- A rate-limited or empty releases fetch returning `[]`, indistinguishable from a real empty list: closed by TC-511, which classifies GitHub errors (401 stops loudly, 403/secondary-limit backs off) and by D-02's tri-state present/not_present/fetch_failed sidecar state.
- Session loss across a restart, invisible to any single-probe-per-tool suite: closed by TC-542 (honest session-loss behaviour) and TC-564 (sidecar read-once and tri-state fixes, plus a read timeout on stalled connections).

## 5. What was verified good

### Slice Generation, readiness and recovery (GRR)

`/healthz` is unconditional and `/readyz` reads real content; generations are read per call, and an atomic pointer flip is served at once and self-heals. Bad ingestion cannot destroy the last good generation (kills at 3 publish points, flip failure, ENOSPC, and guard refusals were all tested safe). `rollback.py` works. Restart and `kill -9` recover in about 1.2 seconds. A stale session answers 404. Path traversal in pointer ids is rejected. All 40 baked scopes (now 32) were self-consistent, and `demo_entrypoint` fails fast on a bad configuration.

### Slice Fleet and Kubernetes (FK8S)

Identity (pod, release, generation, pointer, product-reference platform) agreed for all 40 pilots; live generations were content-identical to the baked demo-manifests for all 40. Restarts took 13 to 14 seconds with identical data, and a whole-cluster bounce recovered unaided. `/healthz` is pure liveness and `/readyz` is a real round trip. Pods ran as `runAsNonRoot` uid 10001 with a read-only root filesystem, no privilege escalation and every capability dropped across 81 containers; no `hostPath`, `hostNetwork` or `privileged` pod existed. Services were `ClusterIP` only behind per-release NetworkPolicies, the default ServiceAccount had no bindings, `GITHUB_TOKEN` appeared only in ingestion Jobs, serving RSS held around 90 MB, and the 15 empty-release pilots were independently confirmed to have 0 upstream releases rather than a fetch failure.

### Slice Protocol and security (PS)

No P0 of any kind: no cross-scope leak, no fabricated API, and no path, traceback or secret leak across roughly 770 sampled responses and 17,000-plus load calls. Scope stayed fixed under header, `_meta` and extra-parameter manipulation; input validation rejected extras with `extra=forbid`; a cross-pilot session id answered 404. Behaviour was deterministic across calls, sessions and transports. Concurrency held 600 of 600 at 20x30 with 1,609 unique correlation ids; warm latency ran 11 to 35 ms p50; adversarial 1,000-character queries answered in 6 to 195 ms; an roughly 17,000-call soak held memory in a 93 to 97 MiB plateau inside the 256 MiB/0.5 CPU budget with no OOM. Request limits, protocol-version negotiation, Origin rejection, content-type checks, batch rejection and log-injection resistance all held. HTTP and stdio answered identically across 7 pilots and 33 calls each with zero differences, and an out-of-scope intent got an honest miss.

### Slice Isolation and provenance (ISOPROV)

No cross-scope leak, no exact match of another product's symbol, no misleading near-miss and no scope-changing bypass, across a matrix of 103,828 requests against 40 targets (19,056 same-family, 27,408 same-platform, 54,936 other): 0 leaks, with only 2 false-positive flags later explained as upstream release prose, and the leak checker itself validated by negative and positive controls. 4,098 bypass requests on 10 pilots (argument lures, header, URL, `_meta` and session-replay manipulation, natural-language redirection, injection strings) all held scope; 1,440 responses were byte-identical to baseline. A scan of all 7,783 baked chunks for injection patterns found none. Re-running the extractor on HEAD fixtures reproduced 6,839 of 6,840 baked symbol chunks byte-for-byte; every scope had exactly one generation with a matching pointer; 21 upstream clones at their recorded commits confirmed all 3,258 sampled indexed symbols exist in a real file (0 fabricated); test-file filtering was correct; and install messages for not-on-registry packages were honest.

### Slice Agentic tasks (AGT)

The MCP server served no wrong or fabricated signature across all 1,037 calls of the 65 blind tasks; every wrong API an agent wrote in blind code traced to missing information or a forced guess, never to a false statement from the server. 39 of 65 tasks (60%) were usable as served (fully sufficient or sufficient with minor inference), and the review's own acceptance test was to re-run the same 65 blind tasks and see the incomplete, undiscoverable, stale and incorrect ones move mostly to minor inference.

### Slice Examples, product reference and releases (D)

Input validation held on all four tools (section enum case-sensitivity, `limit` 1-100, `query` 1-1000, `top_k` 1-100, commit-hash tolerance for case and padding, `additionalProperties: false`); an exact `Example: <title>` lookup always returned `source=exact` at coverage 1.0; `report_index_freshness` never claimed freshness without evidence; all 27 served `contributing`/`agent_guidance` chunks were flagged untrusted; releases (tags, order, `published_at`, body) were byte-identical to GitHub for all 55 served releases across 40 pilots; support URLs answered HTTP 200 for 40 of 40 pilots; 34 of 34 registry verdicts and compatibility for 11 pilots were re-verified; 33 of 33 served examples compiled or ran in assembled form at their pinned commits across 16 pilots and 7 languages; no example was fabricated; the standalone image's example-search mode reporting was truthful; and all 15 empty-release lists were truthful.

### Slice Retrieval and source truth (RST)

Retrieval mechanics were sound: input validation, scope pinning, exact and case-insensitive name lookup, and honest typed misses with did-you-mean suggestions all held, and where a type was indexed a README identifier was never missed. Several extractions were already close to upstream-exact at review time: cells/net methods 143 of 143 and properties 432 of 432; cells/java 1,451 of 1,451 (99.7%); cells/rust 1,693 of 1,693; cells/go 23 of 23 functions and 15 of 15 methods; 3d/java 97.7%; pdf/java 96.9%; pdf/net 91.5%; jmap/python 100% of types (the jmap family was later removed from the product). Inheritance flattening with an '(inherited from X)' marker worked correctly (pdf/net 105 of 108, pdf/java 80 of 96, 3d/java 128 of 140, pdf/cpp 99 of 107); typed misses with did-you-mean rescued about 70% of typos and prefixes; `get_symbol` carried a coverage note; a miss never silently widened scope; and no cross-pilot leakage was found.
